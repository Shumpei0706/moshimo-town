# Moshimo Town — current concept

## One line

Rehearse one change before rearranging your coffee shop.

## Audience and decision

An adult owner/planner of a small coffee stand wants to inspect a working arrangement before physically moving equipment. The product makes one proposed change visible and testable under a repeatable morning. It is a sandbox, not a calibrated operations consultant.

## Main experience

Arrange five stations → open the first morning → ask the original Muse companion for one move → see an unapplied outline and actual calculated walking/cups → accept or keep your layout → replay before/after on one clock → repeat. Manual changes remain possible throughout. The initial challenge fixes56cups/300m goals with92visitors/70stock/1worker/¥500/seed731.

## Why this scope

The concept began as a geographic shop-opening what-if. During development we narrowed it because public location data could not substantiate the demand and sales claims an attractive city simulation might suggest. The actionable variable became equipment arrangement: synthetic arrivals and work assumptions stay visible and fixed, while paths, equipment reservations and service outcomes are independently computed. This is an honest explanation of the design decision, not evidence from customer research.

The final product offers one shop interior. No location selection, neighborhood art, geographic datasets or demand inference is part of its runtime. Historical implementation remains on a saved development branch, outside the final distribution.

## Meta and experience

Muse Spark chooses what to inspect, which valid placements to simulate and which calculated result to recommend or decline. It does not invent numerical improvements. Muse Image supplies three original adult-barista guide poses; real pipeline stages change pose and progress. Do not imply the guide is Meta’s official mascot or that a displayed stage reveals hidden reasoning.
